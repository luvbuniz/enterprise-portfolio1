01 · The Problem

Too many hats, one person

I run Stackadoo, Buni, real estate paperwork, a job and gig search, and content. By myself.

That meant constant context-switching. Every switch cost me focus, and things slipped through the cracks: an email with a deadline, a bug report, a gig that closed before I saw it.

I didn't need one giant AI assistant. I needed a small team where each member has one job and clear rules.

02 · The Setup

One organizer, seven specialists, one human gate

Riley (Organizer) sits in the middle and routes work. Each specialist owns one kind of job and follows written rules. Scheduled automated workflows handle the daily checks. Riley only brings me in when a decision is needed.

  • Every agent has one owner job and written rules.
  • Nothing is sent, posted, merged, or published without my approval.
  • Markdown checklists are the source of truth, so any agent (or person) can pick up where another left off.

03 · The Steps

How it runs, step by step

  1. List the recurring jobs eating my week. Inbox, job search, app testing, fixes, paperwork, posts.
  2. Give each job one owner agent with written rules. One job per agent keeps them predictable.
  3. Put an organizer in the middle so agents don't step on each other.
  4. Add scheduled routines: daily trackers, reminders, and cleanups.
  5. Require my approval before anything leaves the building.
  6. Review weekly and trim agents that don't earn their keep. I'd rather add a skill to an existing agent than add a new one.
